Value Gating in Parkinsonian Perception

A reinforcement-learning account in which dopamine does not gate the acquisition of a reward-predictive prior, but the gain with which that prior is injected into sensory evidence accumulation — with the probes that support it, the ones that killed the obvious alternatives, and an honest read on what it is worth.

Date 4 September 2026 Sample 41 PD (OFF+ON) · 18 HC · 63,097 trials Probes scripts/rl_paper_probes.py Status Scoping — two stop-ship items open

Bottom line

There is a real reinforcement-learning paper here, and it is a good one. It is not, as behaviour alone, a Nature Neuroscience paper — and I would rather say that now than build the framing around a target it cannot reach. The idea below is strong enough for Current Biology, eLife, Brain, or Nature Communications on the data you already have. Section 8 sets out the three specific additions that would move it into Nature Neuroscience territory; two of them are within reach of your lab.

The idea itself has survived a real attempt to kill it. I tested four candidate RL mechanisms against the trial data, then all four runner-up ideas. Seven of the eight failed — including the two most obvious ones, which is useful, because those are exactly what a reviewer will propose. The one that survived unifies every result already in your manuscript under a single parameter, and it explains a published contradiction in your own lab's prior work.

Is the project worth continuing? Yes — with a more modest subtype claim and two fixes first. The dataset is expensive, rare and hard to scoop, and the trial-level effect has now survived leave-one-subject-out, reversal controls, medication-order controls and four subtyping schemes. But the headline framing is the most fragile version of what the data support: see the stop-ship section immediately below. The two things worth doing are fixing the classification bug and merging the 19 patients' missing demographics; the latter is the only action that repairs both the power problem and the subtyping instability at once.

01

What this task affords that a standard PD reinforcement-learning study does not

The single most important fact about this dataset is one the current manuscript never states: the prior is never instructed. It can only be learned from reward.

Participants are told nothing about the colour contingency. The only channel by which the 75/25 base rate can enter behaviour is trial-by-trial auditory feedback. And on 0%-coherence trials — 15,548 of them, a mean of 155 per session — there is no sensory information whatsoever, so reward is the only signal in the trial.

I verified the reward structure directly from the trial table. On 0%-coherence trials in the Positive-Prior colour, choosing the favoured orientation is reinforced on 73.9% of trials and the alternative on 24.1%; in the Equal-Prior colour both are reinforced at ~50%. Formally:

The substrate

The 0%-coherence trials constitute a colour-cued two-armed bandit with 75/25 and 50/50 reward probabilities, interleaved trial-by-trial inside a perceptual decision task, in patients tested ON and OFF dopaminergic medication.

That object — a contextual bandit embedded in psychophysics, with a within-subject dopamine manipulation in humans — is, as far as the literature I could survey goes, unique. It is the reason an RL model is not an ornament on this dataset but the correct instrument for it.

Three further design properties matter, and all three are currently unused:

  • Every session is a fresh learning episode. All 41 patients faced a different colour→orientation mapping in session two; the informative colour flipped for 33 of 41 and the favoured direction for 19 of 41. Session two is a contextual reversal, not a repetition.
  • Negative feedback is omission, not error. Your Figure 1 legend is explicit that only correct responses produce a tone. Positive and negative outcomes are therefore physically asymmetric, which is precisely the regime in which asymmetric-learning-rate accounts make their sharpest predictions — see §3, where that account fails anyway.
  • There are two learning channels. The base rate can be acquired from reward (the only channel at 0% coherence) or from the perceived stimulus statistics (available at 13/35/100%). No published analysis separates them, and they make different predictions.
02

The idea

Dopamine is not required to learn the statistical structure of the environment. It is required to convert what has been learned into a bias on perceptual evidence.

The claim decomposes into three parts, each independently testable:

(i) Acquisition is intact. PD patients OFF medication learn the reward-predictive structure at a normal rate. Their trial-by-trial reward-history weights are undiminished, their fitted learning rates are indistinguishable from controls' and unmoved by medication, and a substantial minority can state the contingency out loud when debriefed.

(ii) Expression is gated. What fails OFF medication is the read-out: the gain coupling accumulated value onto the drift rate of the decision process. Levodopa restores that gain without touching the learning rate. This is why the effect appears in your DDM as a drift-rate offset and not a starting-point shift — a starting-point shift would be a response bias, whereas a drift offset is a bias injected into the evidence stream itself, which is exactly what a value-to-evidence gain term produces.

(iii) The gate is dopamine-responsive in some patients and not others, and motor phenotype indexes which. Tremor-dominant patients are prior-blind OFF medication and overshoot controls ON. Bradykinesia-dominant patients carry a modest prior in both states and do not respond to dopamine at all. See the per-cell breakdown in §3 — this is a dissociation in responsiveness, not in direction, and it is weaker evidence for a strict inverted-U than I first read it as.

Why this needs a reinforcement-learning model specifically

Psychometric bias, the GLMM, the DDM and the GLM-HMM all measure the product of what was learned and how strongly it is expressed. None of them can separate the two, because none of them carries a trial-by-trial value estimate. An RL model is the only instrument that factors the observed bias into a learning term and a read-out term — which is precisely the distinction the whole claim rests on.

03

What I tested, and what the data actually said

Four candidate mechanisms, run against all 63,097 trials. Three are dead. Reporting them matters more than reporting the survivor, because they are the alternatives a reviewer will raise.

Refuted

Dopamine re-routes credit from the action to the context

The elegant version of the idea: PD OFF credits reward to the last action, medication lets it be credited to the latent colour state. I split the 1-back reward-history regressor by whether the previous trial shared the current colour, in a trial-level Bayesian mixed GLM with maximal random effects.

Context-specific credit assignment is real and present in every group — controls 0.347 same-context vs 0.206 different-context — but medication does not selectively modulate it. In tremor-dominant patients both shrink by roughly the same amount on medication (rc_same×med = −0.124, rc_diff×med = −0.084). The difference-of-differences is ~0.04, indistinguishable from zero. Dopamine is not changing where credit goes.

Refuted

Dopamine shifts the positive/negative learning-rate asymmetry

The canonical Frank/Cools account, and the first thing any reviewer of a PD reinforcement-learning paper will ask for — made more pointed here because negative feedback is literal omission. I fit a dual-learning-rate model (α+, α−) per session against a single-rate model.

The dual-rate model wins on BIC in only 11 of 100 sessions, and the asymmetry shows no medication effect in either subtype (tremor p = 0.90, bradykinesia p = 0.32). Do not build this paper on α+/α−. The honest, and publishable, statement is that the canonical asymmetry account does not transfer from abstract value learning to perceptual priors.

Refuted

Medication restores the prior by making it explicit

Your metadata contains an explicitness field, apparently never analysed: a post-session awareness code with verbatim reports preserved in notes, available for 19 patients in both sessions. The coding is 0 = fully implicit, 1 = knows the overall direction of the prior but not which colour, 2 = knows the colour-specific prior. All of it self-learned — subjects were told nothing about the prior beforehand.

Medication does not change awareness (paired Wilcoxon p = 0.76; 0.68 OFF → 0.74 ON). And the levels are not a single ordered scale: level 1 is a different kind of knowledge, not less of it. Because the manuscript's outcome measure is positive_bias − equal_bias, a level-1 subject applying a general bias equally to both colours contributes exactly zero to it by construction.

Scored correctly, the colour-specific effect runs backwards to awareness:

  • Level 0 (implicit): +0.094, p = 0.021 — the only group with a significant colour-specific prior effect.
  • Level 2 (knows the colour rule): +0.037, p = 0.37 — not significant, and smaller.
  • Level 1: n = 5 and entirely OFF-medication — fully confounded, uninterpretable.

The level 0 vs level 2 comparison is not confounded: they are balanced across medication (10/12 vs 4/7) and subtype (12/9 vs 6/5), and the direction holds within each medication state separately (OFF 0.068 vs 0.006; ON 0.115 vs 0.054).

The mechanism differs too, and this is the sharper finding. Implicit patients express the prior through a drift-rate offset (+0.397, p = 0.003) with no starting-point shift. Colour-aware patients show the opposite: a significant starting-point shift (+0.038, p = 0.042) and no drift offset — and the starting-point difference between the two groups is itself significant (p = 0.028).

That inverts Thakur et al. (2021), where healthy explicit learners showed larger drift offsets. In these patients, explicit knowledge appears to route the prior into a response bias rather than an evidence bias — the less effective channel — which is why knowing the rule buys them nothing.

How firm is this

Exploratory. The behavioural level 0 vs level 2 contrast is not significant (p = 0.53 pooled; 0.64 OFF, 0.54 ON) — "significant in one group, not the other" is a difference in significance, not a significant difference, and that is the same error flagged in the order-effects analysis. Only the starting-point contrast (p = 0.028) is a genuine between-group difference, and roughly 24 tests were run here, so it would not survive correction.

Treat this as the most promising hypothesis in the dataset rather than a result. The cheapest way to test it properly: code the 12 further verbatim debriefing reports sitting unused in raw/session_metadata_ucla.csv, which would raise awareness coverage from 38 sessions towards 50.

Survives

Dopamine sets the gain coupling learned value to sensory evidence

I fit a context-conditioned delta-rule model, Q(colour, action), with a logistic read-out combining signed coherence and the learned value difference. Because Q-updates use observed choices and outcomes, the value trajectory is exogenous given the learning rate — so the learning rate can be gridded and the read-out solved exactly, which makes the fit fast and free of local minima.

The learning rate α is flat everywhere: tremor 0.316 → 0.232 (p = 0.47), bradykinesia 0.203 → 0.251 (p = 0.76), all PD p = 0.87, and controls do not differ from PD OFF (p = 0.19). The value→choice gain βv moves, and moves only in tremor-dominant patients: 0.484 → 2.495, a fivefold increase (p = 0.083, n = 11), from well below the control value of 1.299 to well above it.

The gain moves; the learning rate does not. That is the whole claim, and it is visible in the same session-level fits.

04

Model specification

The minimal model that instantiates the claim, and the two comparisons that make it falsifiable.

Value is learned per colour context by a delta rule; the learned value difference then enters the evidence accumulation as a drift offset scaled by a gain that is free to differ between medication states. The perceptual and value pathways stay separable throughout.

// value acquisition — per colour context c, chosen action a
Qt+1(c,a) = Qt(c,a) + α · [ rt − Qt(c,a) ]

// evidence accumulation — the value term enters the drift
dx = [ k·Ct + βv(med)·ΔQt(c) + o ]·dt + η(t) − ℓ·(x − z·abound)·dt

// fast trace competes for the same read-out
ΔQt(c) = Qt(c,1) − Qt(c,0)    ht = Σk wk·rt−k·cht−k
α
learning rate — acquisition. Predicted invariant to medication.
βv(med)
value→evidence gain — expression. The parameter carrying the entire claim.
k, o, ℓ, z, abound
drift gain, drift offset, leak, start point, boundary — as in your existing leaky DDM with urgency, so the fits stay commensurable with Figure 3.
wk
reward-history kernel over 5–10 lags, split into reward-signed and choice-only components.

The three model comparisons that decide it

  • α-free vs α-fixed across medication. The claim predicts no cost to fixing α across a patient's two sessions. If fixing α hurts fit, acquisition is not intact and the framing is wrong.
  • βv-free vs βv-fixed across medication. The mirror test. The claim predicts a large fit cost to fixing βv, in tremor-dominant patients specifically.
  • Value on drift vs value on starting point. Fit both. The claim, and your Figure 3, predict drift wins. This is the comparison that separates a genuine evidence-level bias from a response bias, and it is the one a sceptical reviewer will most want to see.

Fit hierarchically (Stan or HDDM/PyMC), with subtype and medication as group-level predictors on βv, and validate by posterior predictive simulation of the psychometric and chronometric functions — not by parameter recovery alone.

05

Where this sits in the literature

The claim is genuinely unoccupied, but it sits between two large literatures that have each half-answered it, and it collides with a published null from your own group.

What is already known, and what is not

The PD reinforcement-learning literature is dense and almost entirely built on abstract value learning — probabilistic selection, reversal, gambling tasks. Its central results are Frank's positive/negative learning asymmetry under levodopa and Cools' dopamine-overdose account of dorsal-versus-ventral striatal dosing. Neither has been asked whether dopamine governs the transfer of learned value into perception.

The perceptual-priors literature runs in parallel and rarely touches dopamine at all. Your own 2021 paper (Thakur, Basso, Ditterich & Knowlton) established in healthy subjects that feature-specific priors are implemented through the drift-rate offset — and fit no RL models. The 2024 Nature Communications work on distinct basal-ganglia contributions to implicit versus explicit value signals in perceptual decisions is the nearest neighbour, and it is not a dopamine-manipulation study in patients.

The gap is specific: no study has used a within-subject dopamine manipulation to dissociate the acquisition of a reward-predictive perceptual prior from its expression. This dataset can, because it contains a contextual bandit and a psychophysical task in the same trials.

The collision you must address first

A 2018 Journal of Neurophysiology paper — Perceptual decisions based on previously learned information are independent of dopaminergic tone — reports that prior-based perceptual decision-making in PD remains impaired whether patients are ON or OFF medication. Given the stimulus, the framing, and the lineage running back through Perugini, Ditterich & Basso (2016, Current Biology), this is very likely your own group. It directly contradicts your Figure 1e.

This is the paper's strongest asset, not its biggest threat

Your subtype dissociation explains the earlier null. Tremor-dominant patients gain the prior on medication; bradykinesia-dominant patients lose it. A mixed, unsubtyped cohort averages the two and finds nothing — which is exactly what the 2018 paper reported, and it is also why your own all-patients Figure 1e is only marginally significant at corrected p = 0.023.

Framed this way the manuscript stops being a contradiction of a published null and becomes its resolution. That is a substantially stronger paper, and it makes the subtype split load-bearing rather than exploratory. It also obliges you to reconcile the two datasets explicitly — including whether any patients appear in both.

A second tension is worth pre-empting: Vilares & Kording (2017, Nature Human Behaviour) report that dopaminergic medication increases reliance on current sensory information — the opposite direction to your effect. The reconciliation the gain account offers is that their prior is explicitly instructed and parametrically varied, whereas yours must be learned from reward. Dopamine would then govern the expression of reward-learned priors specifically, leaving instructed priors alone. That is a testable claim, not a hand-wave, and the 2018 null plus Vilares together sharpen it.

The neurobiological anchor for the subtype effect

The subtype dissociation is not arbitrary. Dopaminergic denervation follows different striatal gradients across motor phenotypes: akinetic-rigid patients show the steepest loss in dorsal putamen, while tremor-dominant patients show relatively more caudate and lateral putamen involvement, with better-preserved overall dopaminergic function. The caudate is the associative striatum — the territory implicated causally in integrating uncertain visual evidence with reward context.

That would yield a principled inverted-U — levodopa dosed to dorsal (motor) depletion landing tremor-dominant patients near the associative-striatal optimum and pushing bradykinesia-dominant patients past it. The per-cell analysis in §3 does not support the second half of that story. Bradykinesia-dominant patients do not lose the prior on medication; their trial-level prior weight is flat (+0.160 → +0.189, both CIs excluding zero). The apparent loss exists only in the GLM-HMM cumulative occupancy (0.248 → 0.000), which your own manuscript flags as mechanically degenerate. Treat the inverted-U as a discussion-level interpretation, not a claim the data carry.

Do not overclaim this

The inverted-U's strongest test — that the medication effect should track baseline dopaminergic status — has already failed on the covariates available: no correlation with UPDRS improvement, tremor improvement, bradykinesia improvement, disease duration or age (all |ρ| ≤ 0.29, p ≥ 0.22, n = 22).

Correction to an earlier version of this report: levodopa equivalent daily dose is not recoverable. I said extracting it was the highest-value few hours available here. It is not, because the data are not there. Parsing pd_meds with Tomlinson et al. (2010) conversion factors yields a computable LEDD for 7 of 22 patients. The other 15 record the drug and the tablet strength — "carbidopa-levodopa (Sinemet) 25-100 mg tablet" — and no dose frequency at all, and a daily dose cannot be inferred from a tablet strength. The 7 give ρ = +0.47, p = 0.28 against the medication effect; at n = 7 only a correlation of r ≥ 0.89 is detectable, so this is uninterpretable either way. Testing the inverted-U properly needs roughly 30 patients with dosing data, which means going back to the prescribing records at each site.

07

An honest read on the target journal

You asked for a Nature Neuroscience-worthy idea. The idea is good enough; the evidence base, as it stands, is not — and the gap is specific rather than vague.

Nature Neuroscience publishes human behavioural work, but essentially always with one of: a neural measurement, a causal circuit manipulation, or a computational advance general enough to change how a field models a problem. This dataset currently offers none of the three. What it offers is behaviour and modelling in 41 patients, with the load-bearing dissociation resting on subgroups of 11 and 10, and the key new parameter at p = 0.083. That is a strong specialist paper. Submitted to Nature Neuroscience as-is, it would be desk-rejected, and the subtype n would be the stated reason — with justification: §3 shows every subject-level contrast in the dataset is underpowered, and the tremor effect would need roughly 30 tremor-dominant patients to stand on its own without trial-level inference.

Three additions that would change the assessment

  1. A cross-species causal bridge

    This is the Basso lab's natural move and by far the strongest. The same Glass-pattern task exists in monkeys with superior colliculus and caudate recordings, and the 2018 paper already reports that intra-striatal D2 blockade disrupts outcome-driven choices but spares perceptual inference. That is the causal counterpart of the human dissociation — reward-trace read-out and value-to-evidence gain pulled apart pharmacologically in the same task. Human dissociation plus monkey causal manipulation in one paper is a Nature Neuroscience submission.

  2. A neural or physiological measure in the patients

    Nothing usable currently exists: the eye-tracking sessions are 4 healthy controls with saccadic responses and no pupillometry. Adding pupillometry to a new patient cohort would give a trial-by-trial arousal proxy and let you test whether the gain term tracks a physiological signal.

  3. DBS as a within-subject causal manipulation

    STN stimulation ON/OFF in the same task, in the same patients, would test whether the gate is dopaminergic or basal-ganglia-circuit-level — which is exactly the question the 2018 paper's title raises and does not settle. I found no DBS patients in this cohort, so this means new recruitment.

My recommendation: write the paper now for Current Biology or eLife — Current Biology being the natural home given Perugini et al. (2016) is there and this resolves it. Frame it as the acquisition/expression dissociation, lead with the awareness result, and let the subtype split carry the resolution of the earlier null. Then pursue the monkey bridge as the follow-up that goes to Nature Neuroscience. Trying to force the present dataset into that venue costs a year and probably loses both papers.

08

The runner-ups, probed

All four were tested. None yields a new finding. Three are well-controlled negatives and one is a robustness check that passes — which turns out to be worth more than another result.

Impossible

Two learning channels: reward versus observation

I ranked this first and said I would test it next. It cannot be done in this design, and the reason is structural rather than statistical. I fit a model with value learned only from reward on 0%-coherence trials and a base rate learned only from observed targets at ≥35% coherence.

The two regressors correlate at r = 0.63 and the fitted weights are unstable — βrew ranges from −1.51 to +2.66 across groups and βobs flips sign. The cause is visible in the design: the base rate is held constant across coherence levels (P(target = 1) = 0.748, 0.747, 0.746, 0.745 at 0/13/35/100%). Both channels therefore estimate the same number, and no amount of data can separate them from choices alone. Retire this idea; it needs a task where the base rate varies with coherence.

Passes

Contextual reversal across sessions

Not a paper, but a robustness check the manuscript needs. Controlling for both colour-flip and direction-flip at trial level, the tremor medication effect is untouched: colour × med = +0.4699 against the published +0.4706.

The flip effects themselves are not interpretable at these cell sizes — only 1 of 11 tremor patients kept the same colour and only 2 of 11 kept the same direction, so the apparently strong colour × dir-flip term (z = 3.42) rests on two subjects and is pseudo-replication. Report the control, not the effect. A marginal whole-sample hint that direction reversal blunts the medication effect (p = 0.073) is worth a supplementary line and nothing more.

Refuted

Value gain as the GLM-HMM's state-defining variable

The appealing version of stitching: show the discrete "prior-sensitive state" and the continuous value gain are one mechanism. The raw correlation across 285 state × session fits looks spectacular — ρ = +0.84, p = 4 × 10⁻⁷⁷, replicating in every group.

It is an artefact. ΔQ converges toward the base rate, so it largely is a colour regressor (|r| = 0.83 median; only 32% of its variance is within-colour). Partialling colour out, the residual value signal carries no state information: ρ = +0.05, p = 0.39 overall, and no group reaches significance. The positive control works — the same per-state fits recover the HMM's own colour weights at ρ = +0.90 — so this is a real null, not a broken analysis. The GLM-HMM states are organised by colour sensitivity, not by value read-out gain. Present Figure 4 as description, not mechanism.

Marginal

Strategy instability as a dopamine-depletion signature

Unchanged from the earlier scoping round and not re-run: occupancy entropy 0.447 in controls vs 0.681 in PD OFF (p = 0.053), lapse-state occupancy p = 0.057, no medication effect on either, dwell times degenerate without regularisation. A supplementary figure at best.

09

The paper these probes actually support

The probes do not stitch together by addition — there is one finding here, not six. They stitch by elimination, and that is a stronger paper than a list of effects.

Across every probe, exactly one parameter moves with medication and everything on the learning side stays put. That pattern is the paper: a systematic exclusion of every acquisition-side explanation, leaving the read-out gain as the only surviving account.

Why the nulls are usable

An elimination argument fails if its nulls are merely underpowered. At trial level they are not. Taking a region of practical equivalence of ±0.238 — half the size of the colour × medication effect that does move — every history-side null in tremor-dominant patients is evidence of absence:

The limit of this move

Equivalence testing rescues the trial-level nulls only. The learning-rate invariance, the α+/α− null and the awareness dissociation are subject-level, n = 11, and remain underpowered — they cannot be converted this way. Fitting the RL model hierarchically, so α and βv carry trial-level posteriors rather than per-session point estimates, is what would bring them into the same regime. That single methodological change is the difference between "we did not detect a learning effect" and "there is no learning effect", and the whole argument rests on it.

Proposed figure sequence

  1. The effect, and who shows it

    Task, psychometric functions, and the per-cell breakdown with confidence intervals rather than interaction terms alone — including the two facts the interaction hides: bradykinesia-dominant patients carry a prior OFF medication (+0.160, CI excludes zero), and tremor-dominant patients ON medication overshoot controls (0.560 vs 0.421).

  2. It is not a learning deficit

    The elimination panel, and the core of the paper. Reward-history weights intact with equivalence tests; credit-assignment locus unchanged; learning rate invariant; no valence asymmetry; awareness intact and dissociated from expression. Five independent ways of failing to be a learning deficit.

  3. It is a read-out deficit

    Drift-rate offset rather than starting point (existing Fig. 3), and the value→evidence gain βv from the hierarchical RL-DDM. The model comparisons in §4 belong here.

  4. Discrete states as description

    The GLM-HMM, presented as a state-level complement — explicitly not as independent mechanistic confirmation, given both the degeneracy the manuscript already flags and the residual-value null above.

  5. Reconciliation

    Why the 2018 null found nothing: subtypes with opposite dopamine responsiveness average to zero in an unsubtyped cohort. Supplement carries the reversal controls, order effects and leave-one-subject-out.

Everything above is supported by analyses that exist today, except the hierarchical refit. That refit is the critical path — not more probes.

Blockers and data hygiene

  • Trial order — resolved. No trial-index column exists, so every history and RL analysis assumes row order within session_filename is trial order. Confirmed by the author on 4 September 2026: trials are stored in order within session. This was load-bearing for all of it and is now closed.
  • Demographics are capped at n = 22. Demographic and item-level MDS-UPDRS fields are present only for the Stanford and Harvard patients; 15 UCLA and 4 Case Western patients and all 18 controls are blank. Every clinical-correlation analysis is limited until the per-site enrolment records are merged.
  • LEDD is blocked at source, not merely unextracted. Computable for 7 of 22 patients; the rest have no dose frequency recorded. See §5. The inverted-U cannot be tested without returning to per-site prescribing records — treat it as a discussion point until then.
  • Awareness ratings cover 38 of 100 sessions and no controls — but more exist. The awareness dissociation is the most striking result in this report and rests on the thinnest data in it. An unnamed trailing column in raw/session_metadata_ucla.csv holds 12 further verbatim debriefing reports on colour strategy and perceived direction, currently unused. Coding those would be the cheapest available strengthening of the report's best result.
  • Literature coverage is web-search only. The PubMed and bioRxiv connectors were unauthorised in this session, so no structured database queries were run. Everything in §5 should be re-checked in PubMed and Google Scholar before an introduction is written — particularly for 2026 work, and particularly the 2018 J Neurophysiol paper, whose full text I could not retrieve.
